Tech Companies Are Trying to Shift the Entire Concept of Privacy (Again)

Privacy, which companies were once happy to claim was all about personal choice, is now primed to affect everyone around you even more than it already did. Meta and Google’s smart glasses can record anything the owner is looking at or use artificial intelligence to describe it. Apple’s* Audio Intelligence feature turns every Apple Watch into a transcription machine that records or summarizes your day. Amazon’s Bee devices take that a step further and can record and transcribe everything. Dozens of other companies are introducing pendants , rings , and other products designed to record, transcribe, or summarize everything we say and do all day long. Privacy has always been a team sport. The actions you take with communication apps , collaborative software , or your cloud backup solutions , have long impacted everyone else you’re interacting with. But moving those choices deeper into the physical world where strangers, colleagues, family, and friends are all now a part of your privacy choices is drastically changing the game. Tech companies want us to accept a reality where one person’s privacy choices (or lack thereof) means you suddenly need to be an expert in consumer tech who understands the capabilities of many devices on the market, how they work, how and if they signal when they’re recording, and what platforms they do or do not connect to. They believe these ambient surveillance devices that work in the background and are designed to look as normal as possible are the future, while they also expect the owners of these devices to use them responsibly and to notify those around them of their capabilities to act within whatever laws their states may have. This is not reasonable, nor how consent works in any practical sense.

Electronic Frontier Foundation · 2026-11-03 · 6 min · 1255 words available

Postal Service Surveilled Protest Activities Online With Little Rules, Oversight

The U.S. Postal Service used social media surveillance to monitor and report on protests across the political spectrum, government records show. The records , disclosed to EFF and other requesters suing the postal service under the Freedom of Information Act, show that the agency’s internet Covert Operations Program, or iCOP, routinely sifted through social media posts organizing and discussing protests with little records of any oversight or legal justification. EFF filed suit in 2021 after Yahoo News disclosed the program’s existence and how the agency used it to monitor First Amendment protected activities. After years of delays and technical failures that led the agency to lose access to a trove of emails discussing the program, the postal service disclosed records to EFF and several other requesters this year. The Postal Service used iCOP to surveil First Amendment activities The disclosures include a series of bulletins created from iCOP, which show that the postal service used the program in 2020 and 2021 to monitor and circulate information about a wide range of planned protests. They included a planned “ Million MAGA March ,” a guns rights advocacy event and counter-protest in Virginia, a nationwide Save the Post Office Saturday protest , and planned protests in front of then-Postmaster General Louis DeJoy’s home in Washington, D.C. Many of the bulletins provide dry descriptions of the events being planned and reference public discussions or events on major social media services. Others seem to imply or anticipate that the protests would turn violent. For example, in discussing planned national and international protests scheduled for March 20, 2021 , the bulletin references potential violence in light of “online inflammatory material.” Yet the bulletin acknowledges that the agency has “no intelligence to suggest specific threats.” This bulletin was described by Yahoo News when it disclosed iCOP’s existence. Another bulletin discussing an ongoing Black Lives Matter protests in response to police shooting and killing people styles itself as a “ National Civil Unrest Update. ” The bulletins also recount and monitor threats sent to then-Postmaster DeJoy.

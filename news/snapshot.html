Reader Q&A: Our money and economics correspondents answer your Budget 2026 questions – live

The 2026 budget has been revealed; how will it affect you personally, will it change the country and has chancellor John Healey done enough to prepare for the challenges ahead? Our money and economics editors Hilary Osborne and Heather Stewart are on line now answering your questions Sign up or sign in to join the discussion. Post your questions in the comments below. southlondon1 asks: Is there anything in this budget for young working people? I’m 27, renting a 1 bed with my partner in London, and barely earn enough money to save £50 a month, let alone save for a house. I feel like yet again the government are leaving out people like me who are nowhere near buying a house, have no children (and couldn’t afford it anyway) and our rent costs half our salary. £100 doesn’t really provide any breathing space. Hilary says: The main measure for young renters was the Your First Home scheme, which will mean you can think about buying a property when you have a deposit of 2.5%, but in London if you can only save £50 a month that is likely to feel a long way off. Unfortunately there was not much else that’s immediately going to help you. The thresholds for student loan repayments and income tax remain the same, so if you get a pay rise you will see your take home pay go up by less than had these risen in line with inflation. The £100 energy payment comes alongside the removal of VAT on your electricity bills, and the planned removal of the standing charge on gas, so that’s a bit more money you will keep. However, with energy prices looking set to rise in January it’s likely you will still see your bills rise. Heather says: You’re absolutely right that as an energy-importing country, it’s just a fact that a crisis like the one we’re going through makes us poorer. So I think John Healey’s message that the government can’t completely insulate consumers, but want to do a bit to help, is probably the right one. But whether he’s done enough remains to be seen - inflation is set to rise further, as you say, and the Bank of England is likely to raise interest rates next week, so that £100 cost of living payment might not look so generous by the New Year. Obviously the government is hoping the Iran conflict will subside, which might allow oil prices to fall, and the economy to bounce back (GDP growth was actually pretty strong in the first half of this year). Time will tell.…
